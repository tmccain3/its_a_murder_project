**Blog**

We are creating a project based on the foundation research question “What factors impact bias when identifying a homicide suspect crime historically in North Carolina?” We will be finding research that highlights methods of bias that lead to false prosecution and the demographic and societal factors that lead to this outcome. This will lead us to creating a model that helps us identify homicide based on demographic factors including but not limited to age, race, sexual identity, education levels, and religion. 

Before building anything, we wanted a solid scholarly base, so we spent this assignment learning how to search, verify, and compare sources. We started with context questions about how homicides are categorized, how data collection differs by race, and how North Carolina’s history shaped its convictions law. Then we built keyword lists covering homicide terms (murder, manslaughter, degrees of murder), the justice system (conviction, acquittal, sentencing), and demographics.

“Federal homicide” and “murder imprisonment” worked best, because they were specific enough to surface laws, sentencing, and imprisonment. Searching “evidence AND murder” mostly returned old books and victim biographies, so we narrowed it. One search surprised us by turning up research on wrongful convictions, which pushed our project toward exonerations.

Our next steps are to find a dataset with demographics and conviction outcomes, choose a base model and three methods, and reach our goal of ten peer-reviewed sources.
